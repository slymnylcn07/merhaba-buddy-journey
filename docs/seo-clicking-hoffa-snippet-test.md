# Clicking and Hoffa snippet test

Prepared September 8, 2026. Production release date: pending approval and
verified deployment. This began as a two-page test after PR #54. The
cross-engine re-audit below also adds one description correction to the
weak-knees guide from PR #54.

Only `metaTitle` and `metaDescription` change in these two articles:

- `knee-clicking-when-walking`: retain walking and bending, add the existing
  squatting topic, and use a natural question. The body still contains stairs
  and straightening sections. The description distinguishes clicking with and
  without pain without promising a diagnosis.
- `hoffa-fat-pad-impingement`: keep the specific impingement topic and explain
  the term in plain language. The description reflects the existing anatomy,
  symptom, MRI and treatment discussion, not a new medical claim.

H1s, article bodies, intros, Quick Answers, sources, dates, URLs, images,
guide cards, CTAs and analytics remain unchanged. This is not an article
rewrite, consolidation, or a claim that query cannibalization has been ruled
out. The only additional article change is the weak-knees description. The
location-chart and osteochondral guides, and the stairs candidates, are unchanged.

The September 7 Bing page export shows 4,191 impressions/80 clicks for clicking
and 595/4 for Hoffa. Its date range is not supplied. The separate keyword export
has no query-to-page mapping, so it cannot establish page-specific attribution.
The original edit did not directly re-read the Google export. This was a
verification gap, corrected by the raw-export audit below. A ranking or CTR
gain remains a hypothesis, not a forecast.

### Query evidence and copy decisions

- **Location:** Google `knee pain location chart` has 101 impressions/0 clicks,
  and `inner knee pain location chart` 57/0. Bing has 1,949/22 and 185/4 for
  the same phrases. Keep the shared location-chart phrase and four anatomical
  areas. Bing's `knee pain diagnosis chart` (1,376/7) does not justify promising
  diagnosis or changing the educational scope.
- **Weak knees:** Google `weak knees` has 40/0; `knee weakness no pain` 7/0;
  `knee weakness without pain` 5/0. Bing has `weak knees` 287/2,
  `sudden knee weakness without pain` 18/8 and `knee feels weak no pain` 14/5.
  The retained title covers weak knees and no pain. The revised description
  names sudden weakness without dropping buckling, fatigue, balance, nerve
  symptoms or assessment. The body already discusses sudden changes and
  assessment; no new advice or section is added.
- **Osteochondral:** the supplied Google page row confirms visibility, but no
  osteochondral query appears in its limited query export. Bing has
  `osteochondral lesion` 123/0, plus small knee/surgery/management variants.
  Retain the explanatory title with the knee qualifier and the description's
  symptoms, MRI, treatment and recovery coverage. This is a Bing-supported
  hypothesis, not a verified Google query-level optimization.
- **Clicking:** Google's `my knee clicks when i squat`, `clicking in knee when
  squatting`, and `click in knee when squatting` have 114, 113 and 105
  impressions, all without clicks. Bing's `knee clicking when bending` has
  43/0 and `knee clicking when walking` 37/3. The title retains walking and
  bending while adding squatting. Restore stairs and straightening to the
  description so the earlier snippet's scope is not needlessly narrowed.
  Explicit pain, swelling and locking replace the vaguer accompanying-symptom
  wording, consistent with existing article coverage.
- **Hoffa:** no matching Google page or query is present in the supplied
  exports. Bing `hoffa's fat pad` has 124/0, alongside small MRI/impingement
  variants. Keep the explicit impingement qualifier and MRI/symptom coverage.
  Do not claim both engines substantiate this test or assume Google demand is
  zero. No further speculative change is made.

The Google positions for the four visible guides are far from page one on
average. This metadata-only pass is not presented as a fix for their ranking
gap. No body, H1, Quick Answer, URL, date, image or CTA changes are introduced.
