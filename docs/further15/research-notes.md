## Evidence approach

Each article includes at least six primary research or authoritative clinical references in its article.sources array. Sources were checked through web search/open during drafting. Some PubMed direct opens challenged the browser; the indexed primary PubMed abstracts were used, not third-party treatment summaries.

Clinical distinctions preserved:
- Laboratory biomechanics do not establish real-world injury-prevention rates.
- Exercise trial programmes do not become universal starting prescriptions.
- A hot swollen knee can require urgent infection assessment even with previous gout.
- Crystals do not rule out coexisting infection.
- Anticoagulants are not stopped without the treating team's instructions.
- A compression sleeve is not a prescribed brace or walking aid.
- Ordinary insoles are not a measured leg-length correction.
- CDC chair-test cutoffs retain the original population and protocol limitations.
- New traumatic hyperextension is distinct from habitual hypermobility.

## Images

60 original AI-generated raster editorial illustrations: 15 heroes and 45 in-body images. Fifteen separately optimized thumbnails are derived from the heroes. These depict illustrative situations, not actual patients, named clinicians, product tests, radiology diagnoses, or clinical outcomes.

All 60 originals were visually reviewed in contact sheets. A leg-length assessment image was regenerated to replace an unrealistic high block with a thin plate under the whole foot. The approved replacement is recorded in image-correction.json. Cane and tai chi captions were corrected to describe the actual image rather than an intended but absent action.

WebP body/hero assets: 1536 x 1024. Thumbnail width: 640. Source originals remain outside the repository. Exact prompts and generation records are retained in this directory; local paths in the records identify provenance and are not site URLs.
